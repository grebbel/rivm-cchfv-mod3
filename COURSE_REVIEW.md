- Real-time monitoring vs. endpoint analysis

#### Amplification Phases
1. **Initiation Phase**
   - Background fluorescence
   - Initial cycles
   - Signal below detection threshold

2. **Exponential Phase**
   - Logarithmic increase
   - Optimal reaction conditions
   - Doubling of product per cycle

3. **Plateau Phase**
   - Maximum capacity reached
   - Reagent limitation
   - Product inhibition

### 5. Advanced Applications

#### Reverse Transcription PCR
##### One-Step RT-PCR
- Combined RT and PCR reactions
- Advantages:
  - Simplified workflow
  - Reduced contamination risk
  - High-throughput friendly
- Limitations:
  - Less flexibility
  - No cDNA storage

##### Two-Step RT-PCR
- Separate RT and PCR reactions
- Advantages:
  - Optimizable conditions
  - cDNA storage
  - Multiple target analysis
- Limitations:
  - More complex workflow
  - Increased contamination risk

### 6. Troubleshooting Guide

#### Common Issues and Solutions

1. **Contamination**
   - Symptoms: Amplification in negative controls
   - Solutions:
     - Separate work areas
     - Clean reagents
     - Strict protocols

2. **Template Degradation**
   - Symptoms: Poor amplification
   - Solutions:
     - Proper storage
     - Quality assessment
     - Fresh extractions

3. **High Template**
   - Symptoms: Early plateau
   - Solutions:
     - Template dilution
     - Optimization
     - Standard curve validation

4. **Inhibition**
   - Symptoms: Delayed/no amplification
   - Solutions:
     - Sample dilution
     - Clean-up methods
     - Inhibitor-resistant enzymes

5. **Low Template**
   - Symptoms: Late Ct values
   - Solutions:
     - Increased input
     - Nested PCR
     - Pre-amplification

6. **Primer-Dimers**
   - Symptoms: Non-specific products
   - Solutions:
     - Primer redesign
     - Optimization
     - Hot-start PCR
